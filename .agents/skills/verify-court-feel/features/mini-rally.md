# Mini Rally

Mini Rally is 6 v 6 with the player in their chosen position. The AI serves, receives, sets, attacks, blocks and digs around them. The HUD keeps the score and says who serves, a hitter can call their set with `1`, `2`, `3`, the setter calls the pass in a speech bubble, and `n` re-serves.

## Sub-features

- `rally-start` puts twelve athletes on court with the score at 0 : 0 and the opponents serving.
- `rally-set-call` changes your set call with `1`, `2`, `3` (OH, OPP and MB), shown on the score line.
- `rally-serve` starts each point with a serve: about 6 AI serves in 10 are jump serves (the server walks in, tosses about 4.5 m up, takes off behind the end line and lands in the court), the rest standing floats, which break in the air (the landing marker follows them a moment late). An AI serve can be an ace but never a serve error (a mistimed one is a slower, easier serve that still lands in); only the user can miss a serve. A jump server never lets the toss drop untouched.
- `rally-your-ball` a serve, attack or free ball coming to the home side goes to the player who gets there first ("Your ball!" and a green marker when it is the user). A ball in the seam between two players goes to the libero or the user, never one that a team-mate is clearly closer to; the setter digs only a ball nobody else is close to. A ball off a block (either side's) that the libero can get to is the libero's, even with the user or a team-mate nearer: it takes over from whoever was sent. An AI libero also takes the other team's free ball whenever it gets there comfortably (most of them), instead of the middle or a pin hitter. Between the user and an AI libero, any first ball the libero gets to in time is the libero's: decided within 0.4 s of the touch (a `MINE!` bubble over the libero when it takes one that was first called the user's), never later; the passers beside the user do not step towards a serve that is the user's. A serve coming down in front of the middle passer (even 2 m in front, after they stepped back for a jump serve) goes to them, not to the user 2 m to the side of it.
- `rally-defence` the AI defenders play the first ball in four ways, named in the contact's `note`: a platform dig (no note or a grade note), `Side dig` (a ball past the hip, never Perfect), `Dive` (a ball out of reach on foot: the athlete slides along the floor and gets back up) and `Overhand` (a slow ball at head height passed with the hands). A ball off the team's own block is chased wherever it goes, and on a ball the one sent may not reach a team-mate runs with them. An AI dive (even at a ball just past the hands, often a `Late` touch) only keeps the ball alive: it pops up a couple of metres toward the middle of the court, not to the setter, for that team-mate to play. An AI setter who arrives after the ball has dropped below the hands digs it (platform or dive) instead of letting it drop. No AI player keeps the passing crouch after the rally (a platform put out for a ball that never came is dropped within about 0.35 s), and none dives towards a ball coming down within 0.6 m of the net or beyond it. A pass by the other team that drops close to the net but that their setter sets must not send the home blockers off the net or into a receiving crouch, and must not send the home setter to the net; only a pass that really comes over (0.25 m or more into the court at the setter's reach) does. A tip, however badly timed, never lands on the tipper's own side by aim (it can still hit the tape). No AI player dives backwards (away from the net) at a serve or a spike: only forwards or sideways; a ball going over them is played on the feet or drops. When the other team's server tosses for a jump serve the message `Jump serve · step back` shows and the three AI passers stand about 1 m deeper than for a float.
- `rally-transition` after a block (or any attack that stays on their side of the net) the AI front-row players who aren't playing the ball leave the net for their approach starts once they are back on the floor; a ball their block sends back over keeps them at the net. A middle never starts its quick from the net (at least 2 m off it). The AI setter runs to the setting spot about 0.2 s after any touch that sends the ball to its side for a team-mate to play (a spike, a tip, an overpass or free ball, a ball off either block), before the first ball is played; only on a ball it has to dig itself does it stay. F3 shows the situation each team is in (`situation: you ... · them ...`: one of theirServe, ourFirstBall, ourSet, ourAttack, ourServe, theirFirstBall, theirSet, theirHit, theirFree, scramble). Players without the ball move with the play instead of waiting on a formation spot: with the ball on the other side the back row shifts along the net towards it (at most 1.2 m) and the pin blockers line up on their hitters; on a serve at them, about 0.35 s after it is hit, the setter runs to the target and the non-passing front row to their approach starts while the passer next to the receiver steps towards it; on their own first ball the back row follows the pass; in a scramble (a ball off a block, a `HELP!` ball) the others close on where it comes down, staying 2.5 m off it. Each AI player's spots are a little off (about 0.15 m) and differ from rally to rally. A block only goes up from the net (within 0.9 m of it): a blocker caught off the net runs in and jumps late or not at all. Defenders reading a set, and team-mates covering a hitter, walk to their spot at the pace that gets them set just before the hitter's contact. A player running a ball down faces where they are going and is upright (not in the low cover stance), and sometimes dives for a ball that is out of reach. While a set flies to a hitter, the others cover low in the real spots: on a pin attack the middle at the net inside the hitter, the setter on the 3 m line, the back-row player on that side behind the hitter on the sideline (the middle back on the right), two deep; on a quick the setter close and the pins on the 3 m line; on the pipe the middle under the block. A soft ball into a well-formed block can come back to the hitter's side (`Off the block`). A ball off their block dug by the cover (`Covered`, the user's too) is a reaction touch and no two come off the same: a slow one goes most of the way to the setter (in system about half the time), a stuff that is kept up pops up somewhere within a few metres of the coverer. When the other team's second ball is bumped up instead of set (and nobody of theirs goes up to hit it), the team calls the free ball before it is sent: the setter runs to the net, the front row drops off the net to their approach starts, and the libero and the back-row outside split the court behind them. An AI hitter under the ball (an early swing) never hits a light ball out past the end line: it tips, rolls the ball to the open court or pushes it off the block's outside hand, or swings through it hard.
- `rally-setter-call` shows the setter's call on the pass (for example `SHOOT!` in system, `HIGH!` out of system) in a bubble. A pass going over the net (coming down 0.25 m or more into their court) is an overpass: the setter stays and calls nothing, and the front row waits at the net. The setter calls nothing on a ball nobody can get to (a shank far out of the court). When the setter calls `HELP!` the libero sets it (a high ball to a pin) if it can get there; when the libero can't either, whoever is nearest and gets under it in time sets it. A backup's set is only as good as the ball lets it be (with little time, far from the setter's spot or after a long run under it, most of all one away from the net, it misses its hitter; only one barely reached is bumped up high to the 3 m line instead; a set to the user is always clean); a ball nobody gets under in time is bumped high to the 3 m line by whoever is nearest, and the third ball goes over from there as a free ball (an AI's aimed deep to a corner or short over the net), never bumped over from under the net. When the user is nearest, it is the user's ball ("Your ball!", the green marker, a `YOUR BALL · the setter can't get there` hint, and the setter's bubble reads `HELP!` with `YOUR BALL · bump it high or set a pin` under it).
- `rally-net` a ball driven into the net sinks into it (the drawn mesh goes in with it, then settles) and drops down by the net, coming out at most about a metre on the hitter's side; a ball hit into the side of the tape does the same, while a ball clipping the top of the tape can still roll over.
- `rally-easier` playability bends in the user's favour (`config.ts` says so where they are): an AI defender shanks at least 6 in 10 of the user's hard spikes (over 14 m/s) it gets to, off the arms within 100 degrees of the spike's line; the user's spike off the outer part of an AI block is tooled more often; an AI hitter misreads the user's block 3 times in 10.
- `rally-point` ends the rally with a banner, updates the score and names the next server.
- `rally-reserve` abandons the rally with `n` and serves again without changing the score.

## How to get to it (user POV)

- On the start screen open Play, choose a position and start with `PLAY`, Enter or a double-click on the Mini Rally row. [Start screen](./start-screen.md) drives this entry.
- Open a quick-start link: `?mode=rally&pos=MB`, with `OH`, `OPP`, `MB`, `S` or `L`.
- In the pause menu of a running rally choose `Restart drill`.

## Driving it with court.mjs

Preconditions:

- A desktop session is running and `court doctor` ends with `HEALTHY`.
- The recipe uses the manual clock and the autopilot for the player's athlete (test hooks). Name them in the proof.

- **Start.** Open the rally as a middle blocker with the clock frozen. Run `court open '?mode=rally&pos=MB&bot=perfect' --fresh --manual`. The state line shows `"title":"Mini Rally · you are the Middle Blocker"` and `"score":"YOU 0 : 0 THEM · their serve · call 1: 1 (quick in front)"`. Run `court state`: `game.athletes` is `12`.
- **Call your set.** Press `2`. Run `court key 2` and `court step 0.05`. The state line shows a score ending `call 2: Shoot (31)` and `"message":"Calling 2: Shoot (31)"`.
- **Serve.** Step until the serve is hit. Run `court step 30 --until 'window.__verify.events.some(e => e.type === "contact" && e.kind === "serve")'`. Run `court events`: the first entry is a `contact` with `"kind": "serve"` by an `(away)` athlete.
- **The setter calls the pass.** Step until the call. Run `court step 10 --until 'window.__verify.events.some(e => e.type === "setterCall")'`. The state line shows a call such as `"call":{"who":"SETTER","word":"SHOOT!","sub":"in system"}` (or `HIGH!` / `out of system` off a bad pass). Run `court shot rally-setter-call`: the bubble over the setter.
- **Point.** Step until the ball is down. Run `court step 30 --until 'window.__verify.events.some(e => e.type === "outcome")'`. The state line shows a banner `POINT YOU · <reason>` or `POINT THEM · <reason>` and a score of `YOU 1 : 0 THEM · your serve ...` or `YOU 0 : 1 THEM · their serve ...` to match. Run `court events`: the last entry is an `outcome` whose `winner` is `home` for a point to YOU and `away` for a point to THEM. Run `court shot rally-point`.
- **Re-serve.** Step into the next rally, then press `n`. Run `court step 30 --until 'g.ball.active && g.ballLive'`, `court key n`, `court step 0.05` and `court state`. `game.ball` is `null` and `game.score.home` and `game.score.away` are unchanged. Run `court step 30 --until 'g.ball.active && g.ballLive'`: it succeeds, so a new serve came.
- **Proof.** Run `court errors`. It reports `"count": 0`.

## Gotchas

- A point can end on the serve (an ace, a shank off the pass, or the user's own serve landing out or in the net) with no setter call in it. If the call step times out, check `court events` for an `outcome`, then step into the next rally and wait for the call there. Which call comes first depends on the rally's random stream: assert its shape, not the word.
- The autopilot is weaker here than in the drills. When this was written, as OH it swung late on every set (`MISS`, `Swung late`), as OPP it made no contact in 40 seconds, as MB it hit Perfect quicks when set, as L it passed and as S it set. A rally result is not a fixed number: assert the shape (a serve, an outcome, a score that matches the outcome), not who won.
- The setter does not set the player every time. In the recipe's rally the set goes to the outside hitter, so `events` may show no contact by `me`.
- `rally-defence` was measured with an all-AI probe (`g.autoUser = true`, the manual clock, 1000 s of rallies): about 7 dives, 2 side digs and 7 overhand passes for every 16 platform digs. In a short recipe there may be none: step on with `court step 30 --until 'window.__verify.events.some(e => e.note === "Dive")'` and take the `shot` straight after, while the athlete is on the floor (about 1 s).
- The call bubble is on screen for about 1.5 seconds of game time. Take the `shot` straight after the `step --until` that waits for it.
- The recipe starts on `their serve`. `your serve` needs the home team to win a point first. Who serves for the home team, and whether the player serves, was not driven when this was written. Add it to this file when you verify it.
- S and L have no set calls: the score line has no `call` part and `1` to `3` do nothing.
- A fast call (`Go`, `Shoot`, `Fast back`) only happens off a good pass. Out of system the bubble says `HIGH!` and the set is a high ball whatever was called.
- A hand-played rally on the manual clock is possible (`keydown`, `step`, `mouse`), but the ball does not wait. Plan each step against `court state` (`game.ball`, `game.me`).
